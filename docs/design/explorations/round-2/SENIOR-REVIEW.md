| The three stacked chrome rows take 45–55% of the first phone screen | Merge the Era and the week into one sticky line ("The Brunswick Years · This week · 3 entries") |
| The sealed card repeats the day header's date | Under a day header, the card should show only Opens and the countdown (needs a D-10 decision) |

| A palette's Loot Drop colour never shows on the colour board | Black, white and gold reads as plain grey. Show the feed and the Loot Drop for each palette |
| Write's Seal section is off the first phone screen in every design | Boards should add Write scrolled to Seal, or the phone sheet should open its sections as rows |

| Avoid | Because |
|---|---|
| Medicine-like shapes (capsules, pills) | They misread in a wellbeing context |
| Floating buttons over text | They hide content in a reading app |
| A signature that only shows below the fold (Nightstand's 24 px fan, Folio's running head) | Friends judge the first screen |
| Mustard gold on light Dusk blue (Nightstand) | It looks muddy and drifts towards brown and orange |

**For round 3:** two or three designs, not five. Folio and Starlight as the bases, with one hybrid (Folio's type and margin with Starlight's dusk mode and dialog Write), each with the hourglass or its own object. Decide the sealed-card date rule first.

## 3. Each design

### 10 Folio: 8.3
**Works:**
- The most complete system in the round. Margin dates, the cloth title page for the Era, a dot-leader week list and the closed book all say "your life as a book" without a single prop.
- Newsreader for reading and Instrument Sans for controls is the best type pairing of the five. It answers Aaron's "slightly simplistic, not modern" note on round 1's Memoir.
- On a laptop the date margin finally earns its space: "Monday 26 Oct" sits beside the title like a book's side note.
- The book Loot Drop shows the opening date once, on its band, and the ribbon gives it a little life.

**Watch out:**
- On phones it is quiet to a fault. Apart from the green buttons and the Era band, the feed could be any tidy feed.
- On this day is cut off at the right edge ("First night in the big r…") and the second item is clipped. That is a scrolling row by design, but it reads as broken in a still image.
- The running head shows "memoirist" until you scroll, so its best idea isn't on the first screen.
- In dark mode the bright green Era band is the loudest thing on the screen.

### 8 Starlight: 8.2
**Works:**
- The best dark mode of the round. Dusk blue (#161B25), not black, with off-white text and a pale gold Loot Drop. This is exactly what FEEDBACK.md asked for.
- "Wednesday at dusk" with the real moon is a quiet, unique greeting.
- The constellation Era is the most original Era page in either round.
- The best Write screen: on a laptop it's a dialog over the dimmed journal, with Seal as Loot Drop and its three quick dates visible without scrolling.

**Watch out:**
- The floating Write button covers the end of the first caption on every feed screen, and the voice note on the entry.
- The light-mode Loot Drop is a pale blue panel with a dotted line. It reads like a diagram, not the night sky. The idea only works in dark mode.
- The trail shows 3 of 365 days travelled, so it looks unlit. The countdown text does all the work.
- The date line and On this day push the first photo down to 464 px, the lowest in the round.
- The light-mode gold countdown passes contrast only because it's large (4.1–4.5:1).

### 7 Afterglow: 7.8
**Works:**
- The most instantly usable design. The tab bar with a filled centre Write, white cards on a blush page, and the day stamp.
- The day stamp ("Mon 26") is a real step away from Instagram's avatar row. It also gives the feed a rhythm.
- The parcel has the most warmth of any Loot Drop: a bow, a tag that swings once, a soft shadow. "A gift to future you" fits the product.
- Sunset rose works as the warm option that isn't orange. It is AA in both modes.

**Watch out:**
- It is still the closest to Instagram. Remove the stamp and it's a social feed.
- The tag's rotated small text is hard to read on a phone.
- The parcel can read as a birthday present.
- In dark mode the pink-brown greys lean towards plum.
- On a laptop the feed is a narrow column with a lot of empty page around it.
- On the phone the Write label looks as bold as the active tab.

### 9 Nightstand: 7.4
**Works:**
- The best first feed screen: the photo starts at 350 px, the highest in the round.
- The hourglass is the clearest Loot Drop anyone has drawn. It reads instantly, it shows the wait already passed, and its falling grains give it life without asking for attention.
- The week headers lay out cleanly, and the dark mode is calm.

**Watch out:**
- The signature (the week fan) is a 24 px thumbnail stack that nobody will notice. Without it, this is a tidy generic feed. Its own reviewer said so too.
- Outfit with Work Sans is competent and anonymous.
- On light Dusk blue the mustard sand looks muddy beside the blue, and it is close to the brown and orange family Aaron rejected.
- The laptop entry is centred with an empty band above it.

### 11 Keepsake: 7.2
**Works:**
- The big day numbers are confident and modern, especially in the laptop margin.
- Ink and red is disciplined: red only on Write and the Loot Drop label.
- The large "28 Wednesday" on Write is the clearest memory date in any Write screen.

**Watch out:**
- **The capsule reads as a pill.** It's a rounded grey-and-red capsule lying on its side. In a journal that offers a Spiralling check-in and promises to be gentle with hard topics (D-39), that's a real risk, not a matter of taste. Redraw it as a squat time-capsule canister with a lid and bands, or choose another object.
- The week's thumbnail row repeats the photos of the cards right under it, which breaks the no-repeats rule. It also pushes the first photo down to 444 px.
- The Loot Drop page's back link says "Loot Drop", not the week, unlike the other four.

## 4. Across the round

1. **One skeleton, five paints.** The shared first-screen budget did its job (every feed shows an entry), but it also made every feed the same three rows in the same order. In round 3, let each design own the top of the feed.
2. **The sealed-card rule conflicts with "no repeats".** In four designs the sealed card says "Written Sun 25 Oct" right under a "Sunday 25 October" day header. Proposal for a D-10 update: in the feed, the day header is the card's memory date, so the card shows only Opens and the countdown. Elsewhere it keeps the memory date.
3. **Where Write lives.** Three patterns appeared: the tab bar's centre item (7, 9, 10), a right-hand pill (11) and a floating button (8). The centre item never covers content and reads as "create" rather than Edit. Make it the default.
4. **Loot Drops are a big step up from round 1.** All five have personality and none uses a padlock. Ranked on clarity: hourglass, book, parcel, star (dark mode only), capsule.
5. **Dark mode answered the feedback.** All five use dark greys, not black, with readable text. Starlight is the model.
6. **Boards.** The colour board is 6,120 px tall with 20 phones, too long to read on a phone, and it never shows a palette's Loot Drop colour. Split it in two, and pair each feed with its Loot Drop. Write's Seal section never appears on a phone board.
